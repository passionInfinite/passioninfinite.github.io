Intelligence we can trust, because we can see inside it

AI is becoming the brain behind systems that read, decide and act for us. I believe that power has to be secure from the inside out. I’m working toward that by opening up open-source models, one problem statement at a time, and sharing everything I learn so the community can build on it.

Prompt in, action out. What happens in between is a black box.

We can watch the agent. We can’t ask the model why.

AI agents now read files, call tools and take actions. We’ve built good ways to watch them: tracing with OpenTelemetry, logs, metrics, guardrails, sandboxes and human review. All of it is necessary, and none of it goes away.

But all of it answers the same kind of question: what happened, when, how long, and which tool. None of it can answer the one that matters most when something goes wrong: why did the model decide to take that action?

The decision happens inside the model, and today that part is a black box. Even asking the model to explain itself is no guarantee, because the explanation it writes doesn’t always reflect what actually drove its choice.

Building security agents at work made this concrete for me. When an agent calls a finding exploitable, or decides to skip one, I can read its output but not its reasoning. For security work, that isn’t good enough.

Secure the brain, not just the perimeter

Most of the security tooling around AI agents works on the outside of the model: filter what goes in, restrict what comes out, watch everything in between. These layers are essential, and I want every one of them to stay.

But I think we also need to change where we aim. The model is the brain of these systems. If we want secure actions, we need to understand, and eventually shape, how the brain arrives at them, not only catch the bad ones afterwards. That is what interpretability is: the science of looking inside a model. It is the domain where I believe I can contribute the most.

Today: we wrap the model in layers. They are essential and they stay. But each one acts on actions after the model has already decided.

Interpretability is young. Today’s tools explain pieces of what a model does, not all of it. That is why every post here shows its method, its code and its limits.

A lifelong research partner, if we can trust it

If models produce secure, trustworthy actions, I believe this technology can change how we do science: researchers chasing long-standing diseases, open problems and breakthroughs faster, with a tireless helper beside them for their whole career.

That future only arrives if we can trust the intelligence we hand the work to. Security isn’t a brake on it. It’s what makes it possible.

What I build in AI

In my day job I lead a shift-left security transformation built on agentic AI: pipelines that take a product from vulnerability discovery to automated remediation, while keeping thousands of developers in the loop without breaking their flow. The aim is to scale it across a large engineering organization, and to get ready for a world where AI increasingly finds and exploits vulnerabilities on its own.

I’m also a co-founder of DayAlign.

A harness for finding vulnerabilities

Specialized agents that discover vulnerabilities, triage them and simulate exploitation, so problems are found before attackers find them.

Agent-to-agent workflows

Using the A2A protocol, agents hand off work asynchronously, pause for human review at critical decisions, and resume without losing context.

MCP servers for developer tools

Agents connected to live scan results, vulnerability databases and risk context at the point of coding, working from current data instead of stale context.

Humans in the loop

Slow, manual security processes turned into coordinated multi-agent pipelines, with people reviewing the decisions that matter.
